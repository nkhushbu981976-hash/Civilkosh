STRUCTURAL • PRELIMINARY CHECK

Column Load Check

Check a rectangular tied RCC column for factored axial capacity, uniaxial moment demand, eccentricity, reinforcement percentage, short-column slenderness and basic tie detailing. The axial-capacity equation remains limited to the short, axially loaded case; the added moment screen does not claim a full P–M interaction design.

Design Inputs

Use factored axial load and, where available, the factored uniaxial moment at the same column section. Moment/eccentricity is reported transparently, but a code-based P–M capacity curve is outside this lightweight tool because the existing input model does not define reinforcement layout and bar locations.

Use the effective length from the structural analysis model, not simply the storey height unless justified.

Enter the design moment about the section axis being checked. Use the governing factored combination from the analysis model.

Calculation Basis

Calculation Basis: Ac = Ag − Asc. Short tied-column axial capacity Pu = 0.4 fck Ac + 0.67 fy Asc. The load comparison uses the entered factored axial load directly against this ultimate axial capacity. Minimum eccentricity is screened as max(L/500 + least lateral dimension/30, 20 mm). For the moment input, e = Mu/Pu is reported as the applied section eccentricity and compared with the minimum-eccentricity screen. A numerical P–M interaction capacity is intentionally not reported because this existing lightweight input set does not specify the reinforcement layout/bar coordinates needed for a defensible section interaction analysis.

Code & Assumptions

Nepal-specific references
NBC 110: Plain and Reinforced Concrete is the Nepal RC framework used as the local reference; published Nepal guidance identifies IS 456:2000 as the reinforced-concrete design basis. NBC 105:2025 covers seismic design of buildings in Nepal, and NBC 205:2024 is a ready-to-use guideline for specified low-rise RC buildings. These references are not substituted for project-specific seismic and ductile-detailing design.

IS 456:2000 calculation basis
Cl. 39.3 is used for the short tied-column axial-load expression. Cl. 25.4 governs minimum eccentricity for compression members; the tool reports the eccentricity from the entered P–M demand but does not convert it into a fabricated interaction capacity.
  • Rectangular, tied, short column only: slenderness must be ≤ 12 in each relevant direction; this page screens using the least lateral dimension.
  • Longitudinal steel is checked against 0.8%–6% of gross area and at least four bars; bar diameter is checked at ≥12 mm.
  • Tie diameter is checked as max(6 mm, one-fourth of largest longitudinal bar); tie pitch is limited to the least of least dimension, 16 times smallest longitudinal bar diameter and 300 mm.
  • The axial equation is for axial compression. The moment input is used only to report demand eccentricity and whether the applied eccentricity is above the minimum-eccentricity screen.
  • Not covered: numerical P–M interaction capacity, biaxial interaction, second-order/slender-column design, seismic ductile detailing, frame effects, foundation effects, openings/confinement irregularities, and complete load-combination selection.

Engineering Warning

Engineering warning. A column carrying moment requires a section interaction assessment, not an axial-capacity check alone. A PASS from this page means only that the entered axial load passes the stated preliminary axial, short-column, reinforcement and tie screens and that the moment demand has been reported. It is not approval for construction or a claim of full P–M capacity. A structural engineer must perform the complete code-based interaction, slenderness, seismic and detailing checks required for the project.
